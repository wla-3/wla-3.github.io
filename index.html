01 · Overview

One transition interface,
three kinds of intelligence.

Abstract

Scaling Generalist Policy Models with heterogeneous data is limited by the lack of unified, low-noise action supervision. Human egocentric videos are abundant, but only a small fraction comes with high-quality hand-action labels. Observed world transitions offer a common source of action-related supervision across data sources. We introduce WLA3 (World Latent Action Modeling for Semantics, Dynamics, and Kinematics), a unified Generalist Policy Model framework built around representations learned by a World Latent Action Model (WLAM).

WLAM encodes synchronized camera views and available embodiment-state changes into a compact local latent action and a richer transition feature. WLA3 reuses these representations across semantics, dynamics, and kinematics: local latent actions support action-sensitive physical-dynamics modeling, segment-level features directly supervise the VLM through a Semantic Latent Aggregate (SLA), and an action expert jointly predicts latent actions together with embodiment-specific robot controls.

01

Semantics

SLA condenses a policy segment into direct transition supervision for instruction-conditioned VLM representations.

02

Dynamics

LAC-WM conditions world prediction on compact latent actions to learn action-sensitive physical change.

03

Kinematics

LARA jointly predicts shared latent actions and embodiment-specific controls for executable robot behavior.

02 · Method

Learn the change.
Reuse the representation.

WLA3 treats observed world-state transitions as the common action language shared by video, simulation, UMI, and robot trajectories.

01

World-transition encoding

Synchronized camera views and available embodiment states form a rich 1024D transition feature and a compact 32D latent action.

02

Robust representation learning

Cross-modal dropout reduces reliance on any single input, while overlap consistency regularizes nearby temporal windows.

03

Multi-stage policy supervision

The same learned transition space supports physical dynamics, segment-level semantic grounding, and executable control.

Robust real-world manipulation

The full model reaches 81.9% average success across six AgiBot G1 tasks, outperforming π0.5 by 15.7 percentage points. The benchmark varies spatial arrangements, illumination, and tabletop backgrounds.

5K → 10K → 20K hours

Performance rises with mid-training data

With architecture, initialization, post-training data, and evaluation held fixed, average success climbs from 57.6% to 65.9% and 81.9% as the heterogeneous mid-training mixture grows.

Cross-context generation

Transfer the change, preserve the scene

Latent actions extracted from a source trajectory are applied in a new context. WLAM follows the source transition while better retaining the objects, appearance, and geometry of the target scene.
